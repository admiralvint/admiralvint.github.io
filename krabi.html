Nine nights · 4–13 March 2027

The coast of standing stone

Nine nights on the Andaman coast, where the limestone comes straight out of the water and the day is shaped by the light and the tide.

Two people, nine nights, early March. The flights are booked; everything else is still open.

The season

Early March, and the sea lies flat

Early March is the last clean stretch of the Andaman dry season. The sea lies down, every island route is running, nothing is cancelled for weather, and the water sits at a bathlike 29–30°C — warm enough that you stop noticing you are in it.

The price is heat and company. Highs of 33–34°C, a UV index that pins the top of the scale at 12, and the busiest weeks of the year on every beach worth the name. Both are easier than they sound, so long as the walking and the boats happen in the cool of the morning.

This coast belongs to whoever is awake first. By nine it belongs to everybody.

Rain, when it comes, arrives as a short afternoon shower rather than a washout — the published figures for March range from 24mm to 71mm depending who you ask, which tells you how little of it there is to measure. Mornings are the driest hours of the day.

Eid falls in the middle of it

Ramadan runs to roughly 8 March 2027, which puts Eid al-Fitr on or about the 9th or 10th — the second and third days on Koh Lanta. The date is set by moon sighting and can shift by a day, so it firms up in February.

Koh Lanta is substantially Muslim — the Old Town has Buddhist temples, Chinese shrines and mosques on the same street, and Moken sea-gypsy families in stilt houses over the water. Eid is the biggest day in that calendar.

A few local kitchens keep different hours that week and the ferries fill up, since Southern Thai families travel home for it. Otherwise it is pure gain: a celebration that appears on no itinerary, happening around you because you happened to be there.

Worth knowing

Those two days repay being left loose. Whatever is happening will be better than whatever was planned.

Where to base

Nine nights, and three ways to spend them

Nine nights sounds generous and behaves like less. The Thursday arrives at Bangkok at 16:35 and, after the onward hop, delivers you to a Krabi hotel somewhere around ten at night; the Saturday at the far end is already the journey to Bangkok. What is left is eight days on the ground, one of them spent crossing to the island.

Krabi has more than three days of good things in it, so the interesting question is not four nights against five. It is where the nights sit, and how many times you pack.

Krabi mainland Railay Koh Lanta Travel ⇄ you move

The case for sleeping on Railay

Railay is not really a beach you visit. No road reaches it; everything and everyone arrives by longtail, and a day trip lands you there between ten and four — exactly the hours when every boat out of Ao Nang has unloaded onto the same strip of sand. A night changes the arithmetic completely. The last boats leave around four, the peninsula empties, and what is left is the sunset, a quiet evening under the cliffs, and the beach to yourself at dawn.

The mainland nights sit well at Klong Muang rather than Ao Nang. Twenty minutes further north, it is quieter and cleaner, a long calm bay with just enough kitchens along it to eat somewhere different each evening. What you give up is nightlife and cheap transfers; what you get back, in the busiest month of the year, is room to breathe.

What it costs

The Railay night spends the day that would otherwise have held Tiger Cave and the Emerald Pool. The third option keeps them and gives Lanta a day less instead. Neither is wrong; they are simply different trips.

The one shape that tends to disappoint is the three-way split — Ao Nang, then Railay, then Lanta — which stacks four moves into nine nights with four more in Bangkok waiting at the end. That is a lot of packing for a fortnight whose second half is meant to be spent doing very little.

The mainland

Krabi, and the rock it stands on

Krabi is built on limestone — towers of it standing straight out of flat green water, undercut at the ankle where the tide has been chewing for a few million years. Almost everything here is either on that rock, under it, or looking across at it.

Railay & Phra Nang The postcard coast

Railay mapPhra Nang mapReviews

15 min by longtail from Ao NangNo road access

Four beaches on a peninsula no road reaches, under cliffs that made this a world rock-climbing destination. Phra Nang is the postcard one — cave shrine at one end, water the colour of bottle glass.

Between ten and four the sand belongs to the day boats. Before and after, it belongs to the cliffs, the macaques and whoever stayed.

Khao Ngon Nak A dawn climb

MapReviews

7.4 km return498 m1.5–2 hrs up200 baht

Dragon Crest, on the maps and on the signs. A jungle trail climbs steadily for a couple of hours to a bare ledge of rock that hangs over the whole coastline — the islands laid out below, the mainland running away behind, and on a clear morning the haze burning off the water while you sit on the edge of it. Plenty of people who have done both rate this above the view from Tiger Cave.

The trailhead sits near Tubkaek Beach and is poorly signposted; the ranger station is easier to find if it is already pinned on a phone. Two litres of water each is the sensible minimum in March. Opening times are quoted variously as 06:00 and as 08:00–14:00, and the difference matters — the later gate puts the climb squarely in the heat rather than ahead of it.

Tiger Cave Temple 1,260 steps

MapReviews

1,260 steps45–60 min from Ao Nang

A working temple on a limestone outcrop, up a staircase that will make your legs shake. Hilltop Buddha, a panorama over the plains, and — unlike most viewpoints — a real place of worship rather than a shrine bolted onto a view.

The climb is far kinder in the first hour of daylight than at any point after it. Bags left at the bottom have been known to go missing.

Krabi Town & Ko Klang Everyday Krabi

Wat Kaeo mapKo Klang mapReviews

The provincial capital, and the one place on the coast where you meet Krabi getting on with its own life. Wat Kaeo Korawaram stands white and gold above the street; a night market fills up after dark; and across the water sits Ko Klang, a Muslim fishing island of longtails, rice fields and batik workshops. No ocean views, no nightlife — that is rather the point of it.

The Emerald Pool An inland detour

MapHot springsReviews

~400 bahtHours disputed — checkNo food or alcohol inside

Sa Morakot, a spring-fed pool deep in a wildlife sanctuary, turned an improbable green by warm mineral water working its way through limestone. A Blue Lagoon and a set of hot springs sit close by, and the whole thing makes a fine half-day for anyone with a spare one. It is a long way inland, though, and it is popular; the rocks underfoot are slippery, and the published opening window varies by source — one guide says 10:00–15:30, Google lists 08:30–16:30. On a short mainland stay it competes with the coast and usually loses.

If the days stretch

Kayaking the clear water at Khlong Root. Diamond Cave, which pairs neatly with a Railay walk. Muay Thai at the Ao Nang stadium. A half-day cooking class — they’re everywhere and they’re good.

And Khao Sok, two hours away, where a flooded valley called Cheow Lan Lake has limestone towers rising straight out of it. Boat hire runs around 2,000 baht plus 300 in park fees. It is one of the most extraordinary landscapes in the country, and it wants a night of its own rather than a very long day — which is really an argument for coming back.

Boat days

Out on the water

Three boat days is about right, four at a push. They accumulate into a particular kind of tiredness — sun, salt and engine noise — and past a certain point one turquoise bay stops being distinguishable from the last.

The hour makes the day

A bay at eight in the morning and the same bay at eleven are not the same place. Hong Island takes the overflow from Phuket’s day-tour fleet, which arrives around midday; before that it is silent enough to hear the water moving in the lagoon. Everything else about a boat day matters less than what time it starts.

Two ways to arrange it. Early-bird speedboat departures start around 1,250 baht a head. Or a private longtail, which seats up to six and costs a flat 2,500 baht whether two of you climb aboard or six — for a couple, that buys your own departure time, your own route, and the run of an island before the group tours arrive fifteen and twenty to a boat.

The Hong Islands A room made of rock

MapReviews

A cluster of cliff-walled bays north of Ao Nang, and at the centre of the main island a tidal lagoon completely enclosed by rock — hong means "room," and that’s exactly what it feels like. There’s also a 400-step climb to a genuine 360-degree viewpoint, twenty to thirty minutes each way, taking in the whole archipelago and the karsts running north into Phang Nga Bay.

It is the more beautiful of the two big Krabi boat days and by far the calmer; the 4 Islands circuit offers more variety, if variety is what the day is for.

Koh Rok The snorkelling

MapReviews

Two small islands some 40 km south of Koh Lanta, inside the national park, ringed by white sand and water clear enough to read the bottom through. The snorkelling is the best within easy reach of this coast, and it is the day most people remember from the Lanta half of a trip.

The 4 Islands The classic circuit

PodaThale WaekTupReviews

Tup, Chicken, Poda and Phra Nang, threaded together in a single day. It is the greatest-hits circuit, and genuinely good fun: at low tide a sandbar surfaces between three of the islands and you can walk from one to the next across the sea floor. It is also the most heavily travelled boat day in Krabi, and at Phra Nang and on that sandbar you will be doing the walking in company — several hundred of it.

Phang Nga Bay & James Bond Island Big scenery

MapReviews

A drowned karst landscape of vertical islands and green water, and one leaning stack made famous by a Bond film in 1974 — which is precisely why the boats are three deep by mid-morning. It is at its best folded into a Koh Yao Noi day, which reaches the same bay from the quiet side.

Koh Yao Noi A slow island

MapReviews

Boats from Ao Nang, Krabi Town or RailayMiddle of Phang Nga Bay

Halfway between Krabi and Phuket, and the answer if what tempts you is the crossing rather than the destination. Rubber plantations, rice paddies, almost no traffic, and an eastern shore that looks straight out at the limestone towers.

Be clear about what it offers. The beaches are ordinary by the standards of this coast and the scenery is scrubby in places; many of the day trips out here call at the same islands visible from Ao Nang. The pleasure is the crossing, the quiet and the sight of an island going about an unhurried Thursday. It is also predominantly Muslim, and would rather keep mass tourism at arm’s length — modest dress, no drinking in public, and during Eid week it is worth knowing what is open before setting out.

Koh Phi Phi The famous one

MapReviews

Sheer green cliffs, a double bay and one of the most photographed stretches of water in Asia — and in March, the crowd that goes with all three. The kindest way to see it is on the way past: the Lanta–Krabi ferry route can be broken here, which gives you the island without the round trip.

The slow half

Five nights on Koh Lanta

Lanta is a different animal from Krabi, and the change of pace is the reason to come. The karsts give way to a long island of wide west-facing beaches, mangrove channels, jungle hills and fishing villages that have not tidied themselves up for anybody. It grew as a trading port where Moken sea gypsies, Muslim fishing families and Chinese merchants all settled, and it still reads that way.

There is no long list of things to do here, and that is the attraction rather than a shortfall. What the island offers is wild beaches, an hour of sunset every evening that people stop and watch, the Old Town on the far shore, and the boats out to the reefs.

The drive south

Rent a scooter — 200 to 250 baht a day, less if you take it for several — and ride down to Mu Ko Lanta National Park at the southern tip. Past Klong Nin the road turns hilly and winding and the coast opens up on your right. You’ll see monitor lizards. The roads are worse in the north and better in the south, which is convenient.

At the tip there is a white lighthouse on a headland, a short climb behind it, two quiet beaches and a jungle trail of about two kilometres — steep at the start, with ropes in a couple of places. Two hundred baht each, twenty more to park. The beaches are not the island’s finest, and the resident macaques will go through an unattended bag with real commitment.

The drive is half the reason to make it.

Lanta Old Town MapReviews

Worth crossing the island for. A single street of teak shophouses runs along the east shore, with restaurants built out over the water on stilts and a pier that has stood there a century. Temples, Chinese shrines and a mosque sit within a few minutes of one another — the island’s whole mixed history, still legible in its buildings.

A festival that might land in your week

The Laanta Lanta Festival takes over the Old Town for three days — the road closed to traffic, lanterns strung overhead, Rong Ngen singing and dancing, batik and Toi-leaf handicraft, food stalls the length of the street. It’s a celebration of exactly the mix of people the island is made of.

The trouble is the dates move badly. It was 7–9 March in 2020, shoved back to 11–13 April in 2025 to avoid Ramadan, and 27–29 March in 2026. It is traditionally set around the first full moon in March, and with Ramadan ending around the 8th in 2027 it could fall just after — inside the window, or weeks beyond it. The TAT Krabi channel posts the dates from about January. If it lands in the right week, it is the kind of evening the rest of the trip rearranges itself around.

The rest

Boat trips beyond Koh Rok: Koh Haa, a ring of islets with swim-through caves, is the signature dive site. Koh Mook has the Emerald Cave, and Koh Kradan has a serious claim to one of the best beaches anywhere. And Koh Jum, which no day-tour boat calls at, and is all the better for it.

On land: cooking at Time for Lime, whose profits fund Lanta Animal Welfare. The night market at Saladan, which is the busiest town on Lanta and still amounts to two streets and a pier. Khlong Chak waterfall and Khao Mai Kaew cave. And if elephants are on your list, Following Giants is observation rather than riding.

Rooms

Where to sleep

Names below are leads to price rather than a verified shortlist — March 2027 rates and current review scores are not yet meaningfully knowable, and the ones quoted anywhere this far out tend to be fiction. What each entry does say is where it sits, what it is like, and why it might suit.

Two things hold true regardless. March is peak season on this coast, and the good rooms go months rather than weeks in advance — seven months out is a real advantage, and the sort that expires. And €125 a night, about 4,700 baht, buys a genuinely good four-star here even in March. The constraint is availability rather than money.

Where the budget goes furthest

Five of the nine nights are on Lanta, which makes that the choice worth spending on. A beachfront room at Klong Nin for five nights changes the texture of a week far more than an upgrade for one night on the mainland.

One local quirk

“Breakfast included” often means a set plate rather than the buffet in the photographs — no bad thing, but recent reviews are where you find out which it is.

Logistics

Getting from Krabi to Koh Lanta

Four ways across, and in March all four are running. The ferry is the pleasant one — two hours of karsts sliding past the rail; the minivan is the cheap one; the speedboat is for people in a hurry.

Where the fare lands in the range

Tickets arranged at a hotel desk carry roughly a 30% markup. Booked online they come in at the bottom of the ranges above; bought at the pier on the day, the top.

Coming off Railay there’s no road at all — longtail to Ao Nang for about $4 each, then road transport to Klong Jilad, three quarters of an hour to an hour all in.

Heading back north later, high season only: a ferry leaves Lanta at 13:30, about 550 baht, two to three hours, stopping at Railay and Nopparat Thara. Minivans go at eight, noon and one for 400 to 500 baht.

The car ferry itself crosses at Ban Hua Hin onto Koh Lanta Noi, running from five in the morning until midnight with three or four boats working through high season. That queue is why minivan journey times vary so widely, and during Eid week it is longer than usual.

The shape of it

Nine days, sketched

A shape rather than a schedule, built around the Railay night, with two days left standing empty — nine island nights followed by four in Bangkok is not a fortnight that rewards being filled in advance.

1Thu 4 Mar

Arrive, and nothing else

Bangkok at 16:35, the evening hop south, and Krabi somewhere around ten at night. A day that ends the moment it begins.

2Fri 5 Mar

Dragon Crest at first light

On the trail by half six, on the ledge for the haze burning off the islands, and back down before the heat arrives. The afternoon goes to the pool.

3Sat 6 Mar

The Hong Islands, privately

A chartered longtail out before nine, the 400 steps to the viewpoint while it is still cool, and the lagoon to yourselves before the fleet comes over from Phuket.

4Sun 7 Mar

Over to Railay

The boat across mid-morning and a room under the cliffs. Phra Nang for the afternoon, and the whole beach again once the day boats have pulled out around four.

5Mon 8 Mar

Railay at dawn, then Lanta

An hour of empty sand and low light. Then longtail, road and ferry, and Koh Lanta by early afternoon.

6Tue 9 Mar

Old Town, loosely

Eid, or near enough. Cross to the east coast, eat on the stilts over the water, and see what the day turns into.

7Wed 10 Mar

Koh Rok

The snorkelling day — an early start, forty kilometres south, and water clear enough to see your own shadow on the sand below.

8Thu 11 Mar

Scooter south

The winding road down the west coast, the lighthouse at the tip, the park trail, Bamboo Beach, and lunch somewhere with the sea underneath it.

9Fri 12 Mar

The empty one

A cooking class, a beach, a hammock, or none of the above. The day the trip is really for.

10Sat 13 Mar

North, and on to Bangkok

The early transfer up to Krabi airport, an afternoon flight, and dinner somewhere loud and lit by neon.

Loose ends

The things that move

Some of what is above is fixed and some of it drifts — festival dates that slide by weeks, park islands that close for a season, fees that only ever go one way. These are the ones worth confirming closer to the time. Ticks are just for this visit; nothing is saved.
