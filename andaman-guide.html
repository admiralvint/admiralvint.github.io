Nine nights · 4–13 March 2027

The coast of standing stone

Krabi and Koh Lanta — the beaches worth the boat ride, the temple worth 1,260 steps, and the day trip everybody asks about that you shouldn’t bother with.

Two people, peak season, €125 a night. Flights booked, nothing else is. Check everything before you pay — list at the end.

First things first

You’ve picked the right fortnight

Early March is the last clean stretch of the Andaman dry season, and that matters more than anything else you could plan around. The sea is flat, every island route runs, nothing gets cancelled for weather, and the water sits at a bathlike 29–30°C.

What you pay is heat and company. Highs of 33–34°C, a UV index of 12 — the top of the scale — and the busiest weeks of the year. Neither matters if you do the hard things early and book the good rooms now.

Two rules run through everything below: climb at dawn, and be on the boat before nine.

Rain figures disagree wildly — 24mm and “driest month of the year” from one source, 71mm across seventeen days from another. Plan on short showers, not washouts. Mornings are driest.

The thing nobody’s told you yet

Eid al-Fitr falls in the middle of your Koh Lanta stay. Ramadan runs to roughly 8 March 2027, which puts Eid on or about the 9th or 10th — days two and three on the island. The exact date is set by moon sighting and can shift a day, so it’s worth re-checking in February.

Koh Lanta is substantially Muslim — the Old Town has Buddhist temples, Chinese shrines and mosques on the same street, and Moken sea-gypsy families in stilt houses over the water. Eid is the biggest day in that calendar.

A few local kitchens shift hours; resort restaurants won’t notice. Ferries get busier, since Southern Thai families travel for Eid. Mostly it’s a gift — a celebration that appears on nobody’s itinerary.

What to do about it

Nothing, except leave those two days unplanned. Don’t move the trip.

The one real decision

Nine nights, three ways to carve them up

Here’s the catch nobody notices until they’re standing in it. You land at Bangkok at 16:35 on the Thursday, connect onward, and reach a Krabi hotel around ten at night — so day one is gone. And the Saturday at the end is already your travel day to Bangkok. Nine nights buys you eight usable days, one of which you spend moving.

Under the current plan that leaves three full days on the mainland, and Krabi has five things worth doing. You’re cutting two of them jet-lagged, with no slack. That’s the actual problem — not the four-nights-versus-five balance.

Krabi mainland Railay Koh Lanta Travel ⇄ you move

What I’d do

Sleep on Railay for one night. It’s the single change that buys the most, because Railay isn’t a beach you visit — it’s a place you stay. It’s cut off from the road entirely, reachable only by longtail, and as a day trip you see it between ten and four, which is precisely when every boat from Ao Nang has unloaded there. Stay the night and you get the sunset, the dawn, and the morning you leave. It stops eating a whole day and starts being the best twenty hours of the trip.

While you’re at it, put the mainland nights at Klong Muang rather than Ao Nang. Twenty minutes further north, quieter, cleaner, a long calm beach and just enough restaurants to be comfortable. You trade nightlife and cheap transfers for space — in March, worth it.

The honest cost

You lose Tiger Cave and the Emerald Pool. I’d cut the Emerald Pool anyway — 400 baht for a pretty jungle pond on a 10:00–15:30 window, shared with tour buses. Tiger Cave is a genuine loss. If that stings, take the third option instead and keep it.

What I wouldn’t do is split three ways — Ao Nang, then Railay, then Lanta. That’s four moves in nine nights with four more Bangkok nights after. Too much packing for a trip whose second half is supposed to be doing nothing.

The mainland

What’s actually worth your days in Krabi

Krabi is built on limestone — towers of it rising out of flat green water, undercut at the base where the tide has been chewing for a few million years. Everything good here is either on that rock or looking at it.

Railay & Phra Nang Don't miss

Railay mapPhra Nang mapReviews

15 min by longtail from Ao NangNo road access

Four beaches on a peninsula no road reaches, under cliffs that made this a world rock-climbing destination. Phra Nang is the postcard one — cave shrine at one end, water the colour of bottle glass.

Go early or stay the night. Between ten and four it belongs to the day boats.

Khao Ngon Nak Do this one

MapReviews

7.4 km return498 m1.5–2 hrs up200 baht

Also called Dragon Crest, and missing from your itinerary page entirely — which is a shame, because plenty of people who’ve done both rate this view above Tiger Cave’s. Jungle trail, steady climb, a cliff ledge over the whole coastline.

Two warnings. The trailhead is near Tubkaek Beach and it is genuinely badly signposted — at least one traveller gave up trying to find it. Pin the ranger station before you leave. And carry two litres of water minimum — in March this is a first-thing climb, not a mid-morning stroll. One caution on that: sources disagree about when the gate actually opens. Some report 06:00; Google currently lists 08:00–14:00. If the later hours are right, a true dawn start isn’t possible and you’ll be climbing in real heat, so check before you plan the day around it.

Tiger Cave Temple If you have the day

MapReviews

1,260 steps45–60 min from Ao Nang

A working temple on a limestone outcrop, up a staircase that will make your legs shake. Hilltop Buddha, a panorama over the plains, and — unlike most viewpoints — a real place of worship rather than a shrine bolted onto a view.

Go early for the heat. Watch your bag at the base — there are reports of theft.

Krabi Town & Ko Klang Half a day

The provincial capital, and the one place on this list where you meet Krabi rather than tourism. Wat Kaeo Korawaram, a night market, and across the water the Muslim fishing island of Ko Klang. No ocean views, no nightlife, and that’s the point.

The Emerald Pool Cut it

MapHot springsReviews

~400 bahtHours disputed — checkNo food or alcohol inside

Sa Morakot, a spring-fed pool in a wildlife sanctuary, coloured an improbable green by warm mineral water running through limestone. It’s lovely, and there’s a Blue Lagoon and a set of hot springs nearby. But it’s inland, the published opening window varies by source (one guide says 10:00–15:30, Google lists 08:30–16:30), the rocks are slippery, and you’ll share it. With only three mainland days it’s the first thing that goes.

And if you have more time than days

Kayaking the clear water at Khlong Root. Diamond Cave, which pairs neatly with a Railay walk. Muay Thai at the Ao Nang stadium. A half-day cooking class — they’re everywhere and they’re good.

And Khao Sok, two hours away, where a flooded valley called Cheow Lan Lake has limestone towers rising straight out of it. Boat hire runs around 2,000 baht plus 300 in park fees. It’s one of the most extraordinary landscapes in Thailand and it does not fit into a four-night Krabi stay. File it under next time, or under reasons to rebalance.

Boat days

Getting out on the water

Three boat days, maybe four. Resist more — they stack into a particular tiredness, all sun and salt and engine noise, and by the fourth you stop seeing anything.

If you remember one thing from this page

Before nine in the morning is a completely different trip from after eleven. Hong Island in particular takes the overflow from Phuket’s day-tour fleet, which lands around midday. Someone who did it at sunrise says the timing changed the experience entirely. This one detail decides whether your boat days are magic or a queue.

There are two ways to buy that. Book an early-bird speedboat departure — they start around 1,250 baht a head. Or charter a private longtail, which seats up to six and costs a flat 2,500 baht whether there are two of you or six. For a couple who want their own departure time and their own route, that’s the best-value thing on this entire page. The cheap group tours put fifteen to twenty people in a boat.

The Hong Islands The pick

MapReviews

A cluster of cliff-walled bays north of Ao Nang, and at the centre of the main island a tidal lagoon completely enclosed by rock — hong means "room," and that’s exactly what it feels like. There’s also a 400-step climb to a genuine 360-degree viewpoint, twenty to thirty minutes each way, taking in the whole archipelago and the karsts running north into Phang Nga Bay.

More beautiful than the 4 Islands circuit and much calmer. Less varied, if variety is what you’re after.

Koh Rok From Lanta

MapReviews

Two small islands about 40 km south of Koh Lanta, inside the national park — white sand, water you can see straight through, and the best snorkelling within easy reach of anywhere you’ll be staying. This is the Lanta headline trip.

The 4 Islands The classic

PodaThale WaekTupReviews

Tup, Chicken, Poda and Phra Nang, strung together in a day. It’s the greatest-hits circuit and it’s fun, but it’s also the most heavily trafficked boat tour in Krabi — worst at Phra Nang Cave Beach and on the sandbar at Tup, where at low tide you can walk between islands along with several hundred other people.

Phang Nga Bay & James Bond Island If you must

MapReviews

Genuinely spectacular, genuinely overrun. If you go, do it as part of a Koh Yao Noi day rather than the standard tour out of Ao Nang.

Koh Phi Phi Not in March

MapReviews

Beautiful, famous, and in peak season a zoo. Either skip it or fold it into your transfer between Lanta and Krabi rather than spending a whole day getting there and back.

The slow half

Five nights on Koh Lanta

Lanta is a different animal from Krabi, and that’s the reason to go. No dramatic karsts — wide west-facing beaches, mangroves, jungle hills, quiet fishing villages. It grew up as a trading port where sea gypsies, Muslim fishing families and Chinese merchants settled, and it still feels like it.

Set your expectations honestly. Someone who spent four days there concluded it isn’t an island with an endless list of things to do — the best of it is wild beaches, sunsets, the Old Town and boat trips. That’s not a criticism. It’s the point.

The drive south

Rent a scooter — 200 to 250 baht a day, less if you take it for several — and ride down to Mu Ko Lanta National Park at the southern tip. Past Klong Nin the road turns hilly and winding and the coast opens up on your right. You’ll see monitor lizards. The roads are worse in the north and better in the south, which is convenient.

At the park there’s a white lighthouse, a short climb, two quiet beaches and a jungle trail of about two kilometres — steep at the start, with ropes in places. Two hundred baht each plus twenty to park. Fair warning on both counts: one guide reckons the beaches there aren’t the island’s best, and the monkeys will absolutely go through your bag.

Honestly, the drive is half the reason to go.

Lanta Old Town MapReviews

On the east coast, and worth crossing the island for. Teak shophouses along a single street, restaurants built out over the water on stilts, a pier that’s been there a century. This is where the island’s mixed history is still legible in the buildings.

A festival that might land in your week

The Laanta Lanta Festival takes over the Old Town for three days — the road closed to traffic, lanterns strung overhead, Rong Ngen singing and dancing, batik and Toi-leaf handicraft, food stalls the length of the street. It’s a celebration of exactly the mix of people the island is made of.

The trouble is the dates move badly. It was 7–9 March in 2020, shoved back to 11–13 April in 2025 to avoid Ramadan, and 27–29 March in 2026. It’s traditionally set around the first full moon in March, and with Ramadan ending around the 8th in 2027 it could fall just after — inside your window, or well past it. Watch the TAT Krabi channel from January. If it lands, rearrange everything around it.

The rest

Boat trips beyond Koh Rok: Koh Haa, a ring of islets with swim-through caves, is the signature dive site. Koh Mook has the Emerald Cave, and Koh Kradan has a serious claim to one of the best beaches anywhere. And Koh Jum, where no day-tour boats go at all — which is exactly why you’d go.

On land: cooking at Time for Lime, whose profits fund Lanta Animal Welfare. The night market at Saladan, the busiest town on the island — which tells you more about Lanta than about Saladan. Khlong Chak waterfall and Khao Mai Kaew cave. And if elephants are on your list, Following Giants is observation rather than riding.

You asked

Should you take the boat to Phuket for a day?

No — and not for snobbish reasons. The timetable simply doesn’t allow it, and even if it did the prize isn’t worth the crossing.

The ferry that runs this route, the Ao Nang Princess, is essentially one boat doing one round trip a day, and it goes the wrong way for you. It leaves Ao Nang at 15:00, calls at Railay a quarter-hour later, and reaches Rassada Pier at 17:15. The return leg runs in the morning — Phuket 08:30, back in Ao Nang by 11:00. You would arrive four and a half hours after the only boat home had already left.

Speedboats give you a theoretical window. Out at 09:30, arriving around eleven; back on the 15:00, home by twenty to five. That’s four hours on the ground, for two crossings at roughly $70 a head each way — call it €130 for the pair of you before you’ve bought so much as a coffee. And you land at Rassada Pier, which is four kilometres from Phuket Town and a long way further from any beach worth the name. Your four hours become two and a half.

Phuket isn’t a sight. It’s an airport with beaches attached, and the beaches aren’t as good as the ones you can walk to.

The karst scenery you came for is on your side of the bay. Railay and Phra Nang beat anything on Phuket’s west coast. You’d be spending a peak-season day and €130 in boat fares to visit a busier, less interesting version of where you already are.

What you actually want instead

If the appeal is the crossing itself — and it’s a good instinct, that bay is the best thing on this coast — go to Koh Yao Noi. It sits in the middle of Phang Nga Bay, halfway between Krabi and Phuket, and boats run from Ao Nang, Krabi Town and the floating pier at Railay. Rubber plantations, rice paddies, almost no traffic, and the eastern shore looks straight out at the limestone towers.

In fairness, the other view

Not everyone’s convinced. One writer calls the beaches there nowhere near Thailand’s best and the scenery scrubby, and makes the fair point that a lot of Koh Yao day trips visit the same islands you’d see from Ao Nang anyway. Both things are true. Go for the bay, the quiet and the ordinary island life — not for the sand.

It’s also a predominantly Muslim island whose residents would quite like to keep mass tourism at arm’s length. Dress modestly, don’t drink in public. And given Eid falls that week, check what’s open before you commit a day.

Where to sleep, and what I couldn’t check

So what follows is a list of leads worth pricing, not a verified shortlist against your filter. I’d rather hand you a list with a hole in it than make numbers up.

Two things I can tell you with confidence. March is peak season, so the good places go early — and you’re seven months out, which is early. That’s an advantage; spend it. And €125 a night, about 4,700 baht, buys a genuinely good four-star on this coast even in March. Your budget isn’t the constraint here. Availability is.

Money you’re not spending

Your current page has Panan Krabi Resort at roughly €45–70 a night against a €125 ceiling — around €500 unspent across nine nights. Trade up on the Lanta end, where five nights ride on the choice, or bank it. Just do it on purpose rather than by accident.

How to check these quickly

On Booking, set 4–13 March 2027 for two adults, then filter on review score 8+, breakfast included and swimming pool, cap it at €125, and sort by score rather than price. Then cross-check your top five against Google Maps reviews — Booking scores bunch up at the top, and an 8.4 with nine hundred reviews is a safer bet than a 9.1 with forty.

Two local catches

"Breakfast included" sometimes means a set plate, not the buffet in the photographs. Recent reviews will tell you. And when you book the Lanta ferry, Nopparat Thara is not Klong Jilad — Nopparat Thara serves Phi Phi, and tickets get mis-sold between the two often enough that it’s worth saying out loud.

Logistics

Getting from Krabi to Koh Lanta

Four ways, and in March every one of them runs. The ferry is the pleasant option, the minivan is the cheap one, and the speedboat is for people in a hurry, which on this trip you aren’t.

Ferry90–120 min$12–20 eachKlong Jilad to Saladan. The 10:30 runs all year; the 13:30 and 16:00 only from November to April.

per vehicle		For two people that’s two to four times the shared fare. Worth it with luggage; otherwise not.

Don’t book at the hotel desk

Resort-arranged tickets carry roughly a 30% markup. Book online and you land at the bottom of those price ranges; buy at the pier and you land at the top.

Coming off Railay there’s no road at all — longtail to Ao Nang for about $4 each, then road transport to Klong Jilad, three quarters of an hour to an hour all in.

Heading back north later, high season only: a ferry leaves Lanta at 13:30, about 550 baht, two to three hours, stopping at Railay and Nopparat Thara. Minivans go at eight, noon and one for 400 to 500 baht.

The car ferry itself crosses at Ban Hua Hin onto Koh Lanta Noi and runs from five in the morning until midnight, with three or four boats working through high season. The queue there is the main reason minivan times vary — and see the note about Eid, because that week it will be busier.

Putting it together

Nine days, sketched

Not a schedule — a shape. Built on the Railay-overnight version, with two days left deliberately empty, because nine island nights followed by four in Bangkok is not a trip that survives being fully booked.

1Thu 4 Mar

Arrive, and nothing else

Bangkok at 16:35, evening flight onward, into Krabi around ten. Don’t plan a thing.

2Fri 5 Mar

Dragon Crest at first light

Up before dawn, on the trail by half six, down before the heat. Then absolutely nothing for the rest of the day.

3Sat 6 Mar

The Hong Islands, privately

Charter the longtail, be on the water before nine, climb to the viewpoint, swim in the lagoon before anyone else arrives.

4Sun 7 Mar

Over to Railay

Boat across mid-morning. Phra Nang all afternoon, and the sunset once the day boats have gone.

5Mon 8 Mar

Railay at dawn, then Lanta

The beach to yourselves for an hour. Then longtail, road, ferry, and you’re on the island by early afternoon.

6Tue 9 Mar

Eid, roughly. Cross to the east coast, eat over the water, see what’s happening. Keep the day open.

7Wed 10 Mar

The snorkelling day. Long, hot, worth it.

8Thu 11 Mar

Scooter south

The winding road, the lighthouse, the national park, Bamboo Beach. Lunch somewhere with a view.

9Fri 12 Mar

The empty one

Cooking class, or the beach, or nothing at all. Pack in the evening.
